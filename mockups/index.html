Two machines: a guide to tape-delay looping

Mockups at four scales, sharing one token sheet and two engines. The set exists to settle two things before any of it becomes React: the size system — how big an exercise may get and what it gives up to earn the space — and, since brief v2, whether the two-cycles thesis actually holds when you can hear it.

Playback level 0.55

Drag it. The line down the left margin is the tape, and it runs the whole document — one continuous element, brightening with the feedback level of whatever instrument is currently running. That is the one page-level effect. There is not a second one.

The size ladder

An exercise's size is a budget, not a description. Each rung fixes how many controls it may show and where in the document it is allowed to appear. Anything that outgrows its budget gets promoted or cut — which is the useful part, because it forces every lesson to name its single idea.

Colour means one thing

The palette is load-bearing rather than decorative, and it comes straight out of the mechanism. Every accent on the site answers the question which machine is this?

BrassMachine one. Record. What you are committing to the tape.
AquaMachine two. Playback. What is coming back at you.

What these mockups settle, and what they don't

  • Settled

    Distance, not milliseconds

    Dragging the decks apart works, and the pitch glide as you drag is worth keeping — it is what a real deck sliding along a bench does.

  • Settled

    Two output taps, always

    Monitor and record head are separate nodes in the engine from the start, so the two-pedal lesson is a real capability rather than a diagram.

  • Settled

    Saturation on the feedback path

    A tanh shaper is why feedback can sit at unity and turn to mud instead of clipping. Without it the near-unity lesson is unplayable.

  • Open

    How much coaching?

    The S and M cards both talk back to you. It might be one card too many — worth deciding whether prompting is a per-lesson choice or a site-wide voice.

  • Open

    Mobile L

    The bench needs real width. Below about 600px the drag gesture stops teaching anything. Falls back to a fader, or the section reflows entirely?

  • Open

    Does the ring survive?

    The circular loop face in the M is the one idea here with no precedent in the source material. It teaches well, but it may be a second page-level effect in disguise.

  • Settled · v2

    Offset and drift are different objects

    The Cycles engine keeps them as separate modes with separate views, and disables the grid in drift because there is nothing to come home to. This is the site's one genuine contribution to the literature.

  • Settled · v2

    Editorial claims wear a badge

    The canon framing and the two-cycles thesis are ours, not Fripp's. That gets a marked chip in the chrome rather than a footnote, on every page that asserts it.

  • Open · v2

    Two L's on one site

    The ladder said there would be exactly one. v2 forces a second. Either the rule becomes "one per half", or the Cycles engine has to justify the promotion on its own.

Planned, not built

What brief v2 still asks for. Ordered by how much each would change the site rather than by effort.

  • M · next

    Harmonic rhythm is the feedback knob

    The system is additive-only — you cannot remove anything, only wait. So how fast you are allowed to change chord is a function of decay time. Turn the knob, watch the permitted rate of harmonic change move with it, and hear why the repertoire is modal.

  • M

    The tuning as embedded theory

    Two fretboards side by side, standard against C–G–D–A–E–G. Drag one shape across string sets: in a regular tuning it holds, in standard it breaks at the G–B third. Shows why quintal voicings are the default and what "long stilts" means.

  • S

    Not committing

    The two-pedal split on its own, as a card. Record head to zero, play freely over the loop, then fade one phrase in. v2 argues this is the most important technical detail on the site and it currently has no dedicated exercise.

  • M

    Circulations

    A line passed seat to seat around a circle of players — the collective analogue of the delay line, where the loop is the circle and the delay is one seat's worth of time. Reuses the Cycles dials view almost unchanged.

  • XS

    The inline readout

    Still unmocked, and the ladder is incomplete without it. A live delay time or decay count set inside a running sentence, reading as prose rather than as a widget.
