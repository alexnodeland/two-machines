Three notes and silence

Everything you play is present for as long as the decay lasts, which means the first three notes of a Frippertronics improvisation are doing more work than the next thirty. Norman Lamont, who has played this way for decades, puts the grammar plainly: the first note sets the key, the second is usually the third or the fifth, and the third one should resist the obvious.

Then you stop recording and you listen. That pause is the lesson.

Mud is the failure mode here, and it arrives from adding too much too early. Everything is present all the time, so a listener has nothing to grip. The discipline the card is drilling is not which three notes — it is the willingness to stop after them.
