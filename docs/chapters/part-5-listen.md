# Part V · Listen

## One idea

A curriculum, not a discography: each record is placed where it teaches something the
previous one set up, with one sentence saying what to listen *for*.

## What the reader can do afterwards

Work through the records in an order that builds — hearing the technique first bare,
then aged, then as accompaniment, then absorbed — using only legal streams.

## Outline

Ordered pedagogically (page spec), not chronologically. Each entry is exactly **one
sentence on what to listen for** plus **a link out to a legal stream** (DGM Live,
Bandcamp, or a streaming service). Written **last** in the
[§7 writing order](../architecture/content-methodology.md#7-writing-order), once the
chapters exist to point back at.

1. ***(No Pussyfooting)*** — the system heard raw, before the name existed.
2. ***Discreet Music*** — the schematic on the sleeve, audible.
3. ***Let the Power Fall*** — Pure Frippertronics, solo, the 1979–81 practice distilled.
4. ***Exposure*** — the loops inside songs; Applied Frippertronics.
5. ***Sacred Songs*** — the first recorded use claim (attributed to Tamm, ch. 5).
6. ***"Heroes"*** — what the sustained line does under someone else's song.
7. **The Roches** — Frippertronics as production discipline; "audio vérité".
8. ***Gone to Earth*** — the technique dissolving into texture.
9. ***The Equatorial Stars*** — the same instincts, digital rig, decades on.
10. ***Music for Quiet Moments*** — Soundscapes as the practice's late form.
11. ***Discipline*** — the other half of the site: the interlock, ch. 8's object.
12. **The League of Crafty Guitarists** — circulation: the loop distributed across
    bodies (ch. 10).

The "what to listen for" sentences above are placeholders to be drafted against the
finished chapters; each must point at something a chapter taught, or it is trivia.
The *Uncut* covermount CD (Frippertronics compiled by Fripp) is a nice-to-have check
on the running order (bibliography §9), not a blocker.

## Interactive

None. An embedded player would violate ADR-032; a static list is the design.
Conventions: album titles italic, track titles in quotation marks
([Content methodology §5](../architecture/content-methodology.md#5-structural-conventions)).

## Sources

- **DGM Live — Frippertronics** — the preferred outbound destination (direct to the
  rights holder).
- **Tamm 1990** — the *Sacred Songs* first-use claim (his, attributed) and mode
  assignments per chapter 5.
- Stream links themselves are outbound references, not citations, but each is
  link-checked like a source (see `/sources` acceptance).

## Claims requiring the editorial mark

- The ordering itself. That this sequence is a curriculum — that *Discreet Music*
  should precede *Exposure* pedagogically — is our framing; one page-level mark, no
  citations on it (ADR-003).

## Claims that stay hedged

- *Sacred Songs* as the first recorded use: attributed to Tamm, never stated flatly
  (sole authority; content-methodology §2).
- Any "what to listen for" sentence that would assert intent rather than describe what
  is audible.

## Rights notes

- **Host no audio. None** (ADR-032, R-1). Not clips, not previews, not thirty seconds.
  Every entry links out; nothing plays on our origin.
- **No affiliate links** (ADR-033, R-4): stream links are plain links, chosen for the
  rights holder's benefit (DGM Live / Bandcamp preferred over aggregators), never
  monetised.
- No cover art: album covers are copyrighted images; entries are typographic.
- Quotation and aphorism budgets untouched — one-sentence annotations are ours, unquoted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All twelve entries present, in the pedagogical order above.
- [ ] Each entry is one sentence and names something a chapter taught.
- [ ] Every entry links to at least one legal stream; DGM Live/Bandcamp preferred
      where available.
- [ ] Zero audio files or embedded players served from our origin.
- [ ] Titles per §5 conventions: albums italic, tracks quoted.
- [ ] The page-level editorial mark is present; no annotation carries a citation.
