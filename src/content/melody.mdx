# The line

This is the thinnest chapter on the site, and it will say so rather than pad. The
material it needs most — the living practice of Guitar Craft's melodic work — is
carried by people, not by pages, and interviewing them is outside this site's
first version. What can be said honestly is short. Here it is.

Fripp's melodic identity is continuity: the unbroken stream of even notes —
perpetuum mobile — rather than the phrase-and-breath of blues-descended rock. The
foundation is documented in the biography: his teacher{' '}

<CitationLink source="tamm-1990">
  Don Strike "laid the foundation for what was to become one of Fripp's specialties, a
  rapid cross-picking technique"
</CitationLink>
— right-hand training, decades before the doctrine. And in Guitar Craft the line became
something stranger and more social. Tamm, attending a seminar, watched it happen from
inside:
<CitationLink source="tamm-1990">
  "a simple — yet demanding — right-hand cross-picking exercise on the notes D-A-A-A …
  many of these little exercise fragments we were being given one by one would later in
  the week be brought together and blossom into the most spectacular polyphonic music"
</CitationLink>
.

<EditorialMark /> That trajectory — a private hand exercise that becomes a polyphony no
single player is playing — is this site's whole thesis in miniature. [Chapter
8](/discipline/rhythm/)'s circulations distribute a line around a circle of bodies; the
tape rig distributes a line across a span of seconds. In both, the melody exists only in
the whole system, and the individual player's job shrinks to placing one right note at one
right time. The machine taught the same lesson to one guitarist that the circle teaches to
a room.

Hear it. Three seats, one note each, a shared pulse — generic pitch, deliberately
not a piece. Widen a seat's cycle and an empty chair travels the circle; mute a
seat and give its beat to a neighbour with the hit toggles. The line survives,
because it never belonged to any player:

What this chapter still owes: the picking method's own vocabulary, the later
hardware that let a single guitar hold a note indefinitely, and the practice as
its practitioners describe it. The authorised text is on its way to this desk, and
this page will grow when it arrives. Until then, thin and honest beats thick and
guessed.
