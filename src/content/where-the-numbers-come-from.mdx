# Where the numbers come from

The Discipline half of this site is full of small odd numbers — fives against
sevens, fifteens against fourteens over seventeens. Before playing with them, it is
worth twenty cool-headed sentences on where Fripp's interest in cyclic number comes
from, because the answer is documented — Robertson's argument, examined below, is
that it is doctrinally sourced rather than decorative. Report first, music after.

The dates are plain. Per{' '}

<CitationLink source="tamm-1990">
  Tamm: "Between September 1974 and August 1977, Fripp retreated from the music industry,"
  the withdrawal phase spent "attending a ten-month course at J.G. Bennett's Academy for
  the Harmonious Development of Man at Sherborne."
</CitationLink>

First contact had come just before:

<CitationLink source="tamm-1990">
  "reading the text of a lecture by J.G. Bennett the night before the Red recording
  sessions were to begin … This was the first time Fripp had come into contact with the
  teaching of Bennett, who had been a disciple of the infamous George Gurdjieff."
</CitationLink>

Per{' '}

<CitationLink source="robertson-2017">
  Robertson, Bennett's teacher Gurdjieff worked with two structural laws — the Law of
  Three and the Law of Seven — and the second is the one that touches music directly.
</CitationLink>
For the doctrine itself, this site points to Robertson's peer-reviewed treatment rather
than paraphrasing primary sources it is not equipped to interpret.

Here is the part a musician can verify at a piano, and it is the reason an honest
page about this is possible at all. **The Law of Seven** (_Heptaparaparshinokh_,
"the Law of Octaves") maps every process onto an ascending major scale — do, re,
mi, fa, sol, la, si, do — and observes that the octave contains exactly two places
where the step is a semitone rather than a tone: **mi to fa, and si to do**. That
observation is true. Play the scale slowly and listen: five whole steps, and two
half-steps sitting precisely there. Gurdjieff read those two gaps as the points
where any unfolding process deflects unless a "shock" arrives from outside — and
that reading is doctrine, reported here, not demonstrated. The acoustic fact
underneath it is just the diatonic scale being what it is. Keep the two apart and
both stay honest.

Fripp took the connection seriously in his own voice. Visiting Brussels' Town Hall
on tour, per{' '}

<CitationLink source="tamm-1990">
  Tamm, he wrote of "finding therein an expression of Gurdjieff's 'Law of Seven,' which in
  turn is reflected in music's diatonic scale"
</CitationLink>
— architecture, number, and the scale, read as one structure.

Did the doctrine choose the numbers in the music? Here the honest answer is a
question. The clapping exercise in [Part I](/two-cycles/) counts five against
seven; sevens recur across the _Discipline_-era meters; and{' '}

<CitationLink source="tamm-1990">
  a seven-day League of Gentlemen recording session produced a piece titled
  "Heptaparaparshinokh"
</CitationLink>
— the doctrine's own word, on tape. Whether the patterns are doctrinal or simply
convenient is **an open question**, and it stays open here. What is documented is the
interest; what is demonstrable is the arithmetic; the rest belongs to [the rhythm
chapter](/two-cycles/), where the numbers do their own arguing.
